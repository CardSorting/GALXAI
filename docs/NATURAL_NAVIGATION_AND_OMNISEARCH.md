# Navigation and request intent

<!-- docs-scope: current-guide -->

Private maintainer reference. Public discovery follows [product scope](PRODUCT_SURFACE_STRATEGY.md); search results and local history never grant developer or administrator access. This guide supersedes its earlier unmeasured latency, model, compliance and automatic preloading claims. Dated source history remains in Git.

## Public navigation

The top menu uses one order everywhere: Sites, Images, Pricing, FAQ, Developer preview, with account actions in a separate fixed-width slot. Pricing and FAQ point to their homepage sections; developer access remains private. Active links change color and underline without changing their text weight or dimensions. Account loading, guest and signed-in states reserve the same space. Below 900px the logo/account row sits above a horizontally scrollable, nonwrapping link row; at wider sizes all three regions occupy one row. A focus handler brings clipped links fully into view immediately when the row overflows.

`ProductNavigation` lives above the page loading boundary in the root layout for Home, Images and Sites (including Site detail). These routes share the same mounted header while their content changes. Other routes retain the same `Navigation` component in their page; the compatibility guard suppresses duplicate product headers on legacy error/not-found surfaces. Do not reintroduce page-dependent link lists or a second product header. This preserves the header, not the in-memory image workspace after leaving `/images`.

Pending links use Next.js `useLinkStatus` for a small, dimension-free hint after 120 ms and a text status announcement. The content-area loading placeholder leaves the header available; it never reports internal work or invented timing. Its minimum height is one small viewport (`100svh`), keeping the footer below the first viewport while streamed content arrives. This avoids a visible footer jump when the short loading shell becomes a long page. Navigation uses normal client routing without a page-slide animation. The root reserves scrollbar space, declares its smooth anchor scrolling to Next.js, and disables smooth scrolling and header transitions with reduced motion. Section links keep native hashes and `aria-current="location"`; product links use `aria-current="page"`.

`Navigation`, `MobileBottomNav` and `Footer` use `IntentLink`. Links preserve their native href, keyboard focus, open-in-new-tab behavior and Next.js navigation. Rendering a link in the viewport does not prefetch it. A 120 ms hover or focus dwell requests just that destination; leaving or unmounting cancels the timer. Touch navigation works normally without speculative pointer-down work.

The shared `prefetchOnIntent` helper skips the current route, external URLs, auth/admin/API destinations, hidden/offline pages and browsers reporting Data Saver or 2G. A per-tab budget admits at most three unique destinations in any rolling ten seconds and deduplicates them for thirty seconds. It counts recent admissions directly, so crossing a fixed time-window boundary cannot admit a second burst. These are bounded opportunistic warmups, never guaranteed instant navigation. Authorization remains in middleware and handlers, including private page chunks; there is no middleware exemption for prefetch headers.

## Homepage motion and section navigation

The homepage keeps its Sites-led copy, pricing, example destinations and public-information boundary. `PublicHome`, `GalaxyScene` and `GalaxyStory` render the story and decorative galaxy on the server. `HomeMotion` is a small client wrapper; passing the content as children keeps product copy out of that island's implementation. The existing artwork gains orbital geometry and two bounded decorative CSS loops. A quiet star map and a scroll-drawn orbital path connect the later sections; the closing rings settle as the invitation enters view. These are decorative geometry, never progress for a product operation. No new animation package, bitmap, data API request, canvas or WebGL context is added by the motion layer.

`useGalaxyInteractions` adds mouse-only depth on devices reporting a fine pointer and hover. The background translates by at most 12px horizontally and 8px vertically, with counter-motion in the orbital layer. Content and controls remain stationary. It ignores touch events, resets on pointer exit, blur and preference changes, and schedules at most one pointer frame at a time. The scroll layer separately translates by at most 72px and scales by at most 6%. “Send a star” starts one 1.1-second decorative shooting star through a native button that works with mouse, touch or keyboard. Repeated activation replaces that effect; no particles accumulate. It is unavailable while motion is off. Pausing, unmounting, hiding the page or scrolling the hero out of view cancels it. There is no audio, generation or published result implied by this optional interaction.

Below the hero, a sticky “On this page” navigation links to Possibilities, Pricing and Questions with native hashes. Its underline and `aria-current="location"` follow the visible section without rewriting browser history. A synchronized pause/resume button keeps motion control within reach without resetting the reading position. Desktop adds the existing Sites destination; mobile retains the three section links and a 44px motion button in one row. Targets and focusable controls leave 84px of scroll space so the bar does not obscure them. The three benefit rows gain a connected track that fills as the reader progresses. Section introductions and the plan use brief, once-per-mount arrivals; content is visible in the default CSS and does not depend on client hydration. Anchored or focused destinations skip entrance choreography. The existing framework's inline stream-completion script is still required to replace its loading shell; fully disabling JavaScript is not a supported fallback in this pass.

The example picker retains three native pressed buttons and moves a background indicator between them in 280ms. Changing a starter resets the revision. Drafts appear over 360ms with an 18px horizontal movement in the direction of selection; returning to the previous draft reverses direction. A thin amber line and the explicit Revised label reinforce revision state. The live region stays mounted; hidden sizing copies keep all six variants' controls stationary. No automatic example rotation or fake creation progress is introduced. FAQ disclosures keep native `details` behavior with a short opening transition. Press states and arrow movement acknowledge actual interactions.

“Pause motion” is visible above the hero and remembers the visitor's choice for the current tab when session storage is available. Storage failures leave an in-memory choice. Pausing removes local animation and smooth anchor scrolling. Device reduced-motion settings always take precedence and update while the page is open. Reduced motion retains the static galaxy, selected state, section underline and native disclosure feedback. The animation defaults off until preferences are read. Unmount removes the homepage's document-level scroll preference.

Decorative loops stop when the hero leaves the viewport or the document is hidden. Scroll work uses a passive listener and at most one requested frame, with geometry reads before writes; it has no idle JavaScript animation loop. A ResizeObserver updates chapter positions after disclosure or layout changes. IntersectionObserver progressively adds finite arrivals, with a visible fallback when unavailable. Cleanup removes listeners, observers, pending frames and active finite animations. Transform and opacity carry motion; the galaxy's masking is static. These bounds are implementation choices, not claims about field frame rates or Core Web Vitals.

The [scoped design record](../src/components/home/DESIGN.md) owns this homepage's appearance. The patterns adapt [Apple's reduced-motion approach](https://webkit.org/blog/7551/responsive-design-for-motion/), [Material's response timing](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html) and [W3C's pause guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html). The playful refinement draws on [Vercel Ship's responsive depth and scroll storytelling](https://vercel.com/blog/designing-and-building-the-vercel-ship-conference-platform), [Vercel's interruptible interaction guidance](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) and [Linear's predictable action placement](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh). These are adaptations of documented patterns, not endorsements, copies of their implementations or performance guarantees.

## Search and keyboard use

The public 404 page uses plain recovery copy, a static icon and Home, Sites, Images, Account and Developer preview links through `IntentLink`. It exposes no diagnostic log, routing explanation or invented service status. Its search helper searches the explicit Images, Sites, developer-preview and account list locally. Up/down selects a result and Enter follows it. Recent visits use the existing bounded local history. It warms only a stable highlighted result through the same budget; it never launches an API data prefetch.

The developer command menu retains its local catalog, typo matching, category filters, clipboard actions and keyboard shortcuts. Its warmup effect runs only while the menu is open, waits 120 ms for the selected result and excludes action-only results. Pointer selections share the global budget. It no longer prefetches the top three alternatives or calls the legacy secondary router path. Audit catalog entries against current product scope before exposing this menu to another audience.

`BroccoliOmniSearch` retains its existing local matching implementation. Its legacy prefetch method is not used by these surfaces. `ResilientClient` and `useResilientQuery` are separate helpers; their caches/retries are not part of the Sites bootstrap or image polling contract. Do not infer a production latency, an authentication guarantee or cross-account cache safety from a helper name or comment.

## Verification and recovery

Run `npm run test:egress:transport` for budget bounds and Sites response/authorization tests. The local Sites and Images browser fixture scripts check desktop/mobile flows; inspect real navigation, request counts and keyboard behavior in a production build. See [quota strategy](maintainers/VERCEL_QUOTA_EFFICIENCY.md) for measurement and limits.

Run `scripts/verify-navigation-ui.ts` with the same temporary Playwright/Axe module environment as the [image fixture](maintainers/image-studio-review.md#verification-checkpoint), optionally passing a localhost base URL (default `http://127.0.0.1:3417`). It blocks external requests and mocks account/workspace APIs. It compares header geometry and DOM identity across Home, Sites, Images, back/forward, account states, slow navigation and five viewport widths; it checks section links, reduced motion, overflow and scoped header accessibility. Captures and results stay under ignored `.impeccable/review/navigation/`. [September 13 navigation evidence](knowledge/audits/2026-09-13-navigation-continuity.json) records actual checks and limits; browser fixtures are not live acceptance or comprehensive assistive-technology testing.

If prefetch causes a regression, keep native links and disable only warmup scheduling while investigating. Never remove access checks or weaken private cache headers to make a navigation faster. An unavailable prefetched route must still have a normal click/retry path. Public UI wording describes the destination and recovery action, not operational details.

If header continuity regresses, inspect root ownership, the product-route guard, fixed account slots and wrapping before altering routing. Revert the header/root/page-wrapper changes together when necessary; restoring only a page header would create duplicates. Keep the sanitized loading copy and existing access checks. Verify a nested Site and not-found route when changing the product-route predicate.

For homepage changes, run `node scripts/verify-homepage-ui.mjs` after `npm run build`. It starts an isolated localhost production server without inheriting application credentials, blocks external browser requests, and uses the same temporary Playwright/Axe module setup. It checks 320/390/768/1440px layouts, all six example variants, sign-in destinations, unsent-draft preservation, keyboard actions, FAQ, accessibility, rendered public copy, browser scripts and social images. Normal-motion fixtures use a desktop mouse at 1440px and mobile touch emulation at 390px. They cover pointer reset and stationary controls, touch exclusion from parallax, keyboard/touch shooting stars, bounded rapid activation, sticky pause without scroll changes, draft switching, section anchors, offscreen and simulated hidden-document suspension, pause persistence, live reduced-motion changes, and bounded scroll timing samples. Layout-shift entries retain source geometry, with a below-0.01 local fixture assertion for loading and scrolling. A fixture blocking all external client scripts verifies readable content, section links and native FAQ after inline framework stream completion. Captures and metrics stay under ignored `.impeccable/review/galaxy-home-playful/`; local timing is not real-device or live-site acceptance. `--motion-only` isolates the motion/performance fixtures and writes to `galaxy-home-playful-diagnostic/`; it does not replace the full layout/public-bundle review. Earlier motion-pass evidence remains dated separately.

If homepage motion regresses, first disable the affected enhancement while preserving server-rendered content, example actions and native links. `HomeMotion`, `GalaxyScene` and the scoped CSS can be reverted together without changing product authorization, billing, publication or shared navigation. Do not restore hidden-by-default content as a fallback.

The [September 13 playful-motion review](knowledge/audits/2026-09-13-homepage-playful-motion.json) retains the pointer, touch, shooting-star, sticky-control, responsive and performance fixture results. It follows the earlier [galaxy-motion pass](knowledge/audits/2026-09-13-homepage-galaxy-motion.json); both are local evidence with explicit runtime and browser limits.
