# GALX pitch deck specification

<!-- docs-scope: commercial-draft -->

The deck is a visual compression of the executive memo. It should make the mechanism legible in ten slides: a person starts with an intention, directs an agent relationship, produces meaningful work, returns, and eventually pays for the experience.

## Slide 1 — The forecast

**Headline:** More capable machines will create demand for more capable people.

**Purpose:** Establish the non-obvious opportunity before introducing product details.

**Visual:** One person facing an unfinished visual world; the work is open, not finished by an autonomous machine.

**Speaker point:** GALX is interested in what people become able to accomplish through agents, not only what agents accomplish independently.

**Guardrail:** Do not imply that model progress automatically creates product demand.

## Slide 2 — The bottleneck

**Headline:** The next product bottleneck may sit between human intention and agent execution.

**Purpose:** Show why task completion and creative completion are not interchangeable.

**Visual:** `express → delegate → inspect → redirect → preserve → continue` rather than a one-way prompt-to-output arrow.

**Speaker point:** A creator may discover the specification through interaction. More output can create more review and correction if the person cannot direct the system.

**Guardrail:** This is not an argument against autonomy; successful delegation can expand capability.

## Slide 3 — ACI

**Headline:** Augmented Creative Intelligence changes the unit of analysis.

**Purpose:** Define the category without claiming invention.

**Visual:** Four connected elements: person, agents, tools, and work.

**Speaker point:** ACI asks what the person can now initiate, influence, complete, and return to. It is a product and market forecast, not a methodology or a claim that GALX invented human augmentation.

**Guardrail:** Spell out **Augmented Creative Intelligence** before using ACI; the acronym is also used for agent-computer interface.

## Slide 4 — Why now

**Headline:** The first experiment is obtainable now.

**Purpose:** Connect better agent capability to a newly practical product test.

**Visual:** A shrinking production apparatus beside a person beginning a project; avoid a generic cost chart unless sourced and carefully qualified.

**Speaker point:** DreamBees encountered the ambition when capability was harder to access. Current agent and creative capabilities make it possible to test the individual experience directly.

**Evidence note:** Anthropic’s agent-autonomy analysis illustrates that experienced users can combine more automatic approval with more selective interruption. It is one product and population, not a universal law.

## Slide 5 — Founder fit

**Headline:** The founder has studied both sides of the relationship.

**Purpose:** Explain why William is specifically equipped to pursue the forecast.

**Visual:** A continuous path from human-centered systems and creative work through agent construction to GALX.

**Speaker point:** DreamBees, LUMI, and GALX create proximity to both what people are trying to make and what happens when working systems act. The May 2026 JoyZoning field report distinguished generated changes from accepted work and kept completion under human authority.

**Guardrail:** Project observations support founder fit; they are not independent market validation.

## Slide 6 — The wedge

**Headline:** One creator. One visual idea. One meaningful result.

**Purpose:** Make the first commercial entry point concrete.

**Visual:** `bring an idea → direct and revise → keep or share → return` as a storyboard.

**Speaker point:** GALX starts with image creation and community discovery for independent creators with recurring visual ideas. The product must be useful before community scale and when one person is present.

## Slide 7 — Activation and portfolio

**Headline:** Permission becomes action; the product earns the next visit.

**Purpose:** Give HEAV3NS MANDATE and LUMI distinct jobs.

**Visual:** A threshold from hesitation to a continuing project, with a secondary path for software creation.

**Speaker point:** HEAV3NS MANDATE is the call to begin, not a product tier. LUMI is a maintained software-creation instrument, not a second equally resourced go-to-market campaign or a general coding-agent arms race.

## Slide 8 — The business and scale hypothesis

**Headline:** Turn individual capability into repeatable paid value.

**Purpose:** Answer what could become a large business.

**Visual:** `meaningful result → recurring projects → retained creator → preferred creative environment`, with founder intervention visibly decreasing.

**Speaker point:** The unit is a retained, paying creator. Expansion starts with deeper creative work, then adjacent capabilities retained users demonstrate they need. Growth in customer value must outpace growth in founder intervention.

**Guardrail:** Do not substitute the size of all creative spending for a measured market.

## Slide 9 — Evidence and defensibility

**Headline:** The thesis must survive comparison, behavior, and economics.

**Purpose:** Show how GALX will earn a preference when comparable generation exists elsewhere.

**Visual:** Three proof columns: capability, product, business.

**Speaker point:** Measure meaningful completion, direction, revision, recoverability, independent return, payment, cost to serve, and distribution beyond founder-led cohorts. Accumulated learning about intention and intervention is a defensibility hypothesis, not an established moat.

**Research note:** A 2024 meta-analysis of 106 experiments found that human–AI combinations did not reliably beat the better standalone condition overall, while content-creation tasks were more promising. That is why GALX must test augmentation rather than assert it.

## Slide 10 — The ask

**Headline:** Make the first proof small enough to perform.

**Purpose:** Tie capital to falsifiable milestones.

**Visual:** `product loop → bounded cohorts → paid retention → repeatable delivery`.

**Speaker point:** Capital improves the image-first experience, runs bounded activation cohorts, measures behavior and willingness to pay, and builds delivery capacity that prevents founder dependency.

**Close:** One person making something they could not previously make demonstrates possibility. Repeated use, payment, and sustainable delivery demonstrate the business.

## Speaker guardrails

- Use **ACI** for the capability thesis, **HEAV3NS MANDATE** for activation, **LUMI** for the maintained software instrument, and **GALX** for the company and product.
- State the forecast as a forecast. Separate observed evidence, current capability, hypotheses, and next tests.
- Do not claim a moat, product–market fit, market size, model superiority, or universal human–AI benefit before evidence exists.
- Keep topology, shard configuration, credential sources, upstream authentication, routing mechanics, internal constraints, execution-model selection, and diagnostics out of the deck.
