# GALX investor materials

<!-- docs-scope: commercial-draft -->

This folder is the working commercial narrative for GALX. It describes a forecast, a focused product entry point, and the evidence required to earn a venture-scale claim. It is not a substitute for customer evidence, audited financials, or a promise about future model capability.

## The thesis in one page

GALX is building **Augmented Creative Intelligence (ACI)**: a product category organized around what a person can accomplish with agents, tools, and creative work together.

Our forecast for the agent age is simple:

> **More capable machines will create demand for more capable people.**

The next bottleneck may not be whether an agent can execute a task. It may be whether a person can turn that execution toward an intention, notice what is useful, redirect what is not, preserve the decisions that matter, and continue the work.

ACI names that combined system. It is not a claim that GALX invented human augmentation, a design methodology, or a new kind of model. The commercial opportunity is to make the system useful enough that people choose it repeatedly.

The first proof is deliberately small:

> **One person creates something meaningful that was previously beyond practical reach.**

The venture begins when that result can be delivered repeatedly to a growing population without William personally carrying every interaction.

## The mechanism

```text
human intention
      ↓
express → delegate → inspect → redirect → preserve → continue
      ↓
meaningful creative work
      ↓
returning creator
      ↓
paid, sustainable product
```

This is not an argument against autonomy. A person can become more capable because an agent handles a large amount of work independently. The relevant question is whether delegation advances the person’s purpose without making them the machine’s full-time operator.

## The three names

- **ACI** is the capability thesis: expand what a person can imagine, direct, make, revise, and return to.
- **HEAV3NS MANDATE** is the activation: the moment available capability becomes permission to begin. It is not a product tier or a promise to be productive.
- **GALX** is the company and product that must make the thesis repeatable, desirable, and economically sustainable.

**LUMI** remains a maintained expression of the same thesis in software creation. It is coding-agent-shaped, but it is not a second simultaneous go-to-market campaign or a general-purpose coding-agent arms race. Its coding ability serves a person’s creative work.

ACI is spelled out on first use because the acronym is also used elsewhere for *agent-computer interface*. GALX is using ACI as **Augmented Creative Intelligence**.

## The commercial entry point

GALX starts with image creation and community discovery for independent creators with recurring visual ideas. The loop is:

**Bring an idea → direct and revise → keep or share → return with another idea.**

Community can help someone see a possibility, but the product must be useful when one person is present. A cohort or hackathon can demonstrate activation; it cannot, by itself, prove retention or willingness to pay.

Public Developer Access is retired as a commercial wedge. Existing capability may support GALX’s product and explicitly administered private access, but customers are being asked to pay for a creative experience—not a general-purpose inference endpoint.

## The proof ladder

| Proof | What must become true | What would count as evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | A creator can accomplish something meaningful with usable direction over the result. | Completed work, deliberate revision, perceived control, and the ability to reject or change course. |
| Product | Creators return for subsequent work without continuous founder guidance or event subsidy. | Second-project behavior, retained sessions, self-serve continuation, and substitution evidence. |
| Business | Enough creators pay while delivery remains economically sustainable. | Paid retention, contribution margin, acquisition repeatability, and declining founder intervention per creator. |

## Spoken founder pitch

> I’m building GALX around a forecast for the agent age: more capable agents will not automatically make people more capable creators.
>
> There is a product problem between what a machine can execute and what a person can meaningfully direct. That is where we are focused.
>
> We call the thesis Augmented Creative Intelligence—ACI. The unit is not the agent alone. It is the person, the tools, and the work they can produce together.
>
> My background is in human-centered systems, and I have spent years pursuing creative capability through DreamBees, LUMI, and now GALX. Those projects gave me experience with both sides of the interaction: what people are trying to make, and what happens when agents actually act.
>
> One recurring lesson is that generation is not the same as completion. A system can produce more while leaving its operator with more interpretation, correction, and responsibility. I am building for the opposite result: more capability without turning the person into the machine’s full-time supervisor.
>
> We are starting with image creation and community discovery. LUMI remains an instrument for software creation, but we are not entering a general coding-agent arms race or selling public inference access.
>
> HEAV3NS MANDATE is our call to activation: making the available capability feel usable enough that someone begins. Private creative cohorts give us a bounded way to demonstrate that, then measure whether people return.
>
> One person making something they could not previously make demonstrates the possibility. Repeated use, payment, and sustainable delivery demonstrate the business.
>
> Our ambition is to build a preferred creative environment for people whose ideas exceed what their current tools let them do. My advantage is not that I can predict every model release. It is that I understand the human–agent relationship we have to get right for those releases to become useful.

## Selected research context

These sources inform the questions GALX is testing; they do not prove GALX’s product or market:

- [Anthropic: Measuring AI agent autonomy in practice](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy) — one product and user-population analysis relevant to approval and interruption behavior.
- [Doug Engelbart Institute: Augmenting Human Intellect](https://www.dougengelbart.org/pubs/augment-3906.html) — historical context for increasing human capability through a larger human–tool system.
- [Microsoft Research: Guidelines for Human–AI Interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/) — evidence that human–AI interaction design predates current agents.
- [Nature Human Behaviour: When combinations of humans and AI are useful](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1) — a meta-analysis showing why augmentation must be evaluated rather than assumed.
- [Anthropic: Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) — terminology context for the separate use of ACI as agent-computer interface.
