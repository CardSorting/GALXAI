# Standard relay rotation

<!-- docs-scope: current-guide -->

Private operator and implementation reference. [Routing](../ROUTING_ALGORITHMS.md) fixes the pool first; [central account authority](relay-account-management.md) still owns assignment, credentials and account leases/budgets. This policy applies to Standard's free provider accounts. It does not define GALX customer allowances or claim a provider quota size.

## Selection and admission

For each new text request, Standard selects the least recently selected eligible account. One ordering spans all models. Model filtering, projection reordering and temporary cooldowns do not reset the order. Valid engine checkpoint timestamps seed first-use order; missing/invalid timestamps use a neutral value, with stable account IDs breaking ties. After an account's first selection, an ordered rotation map determines its turn independently of wall-clock changes or refreshed last-use timestamps. A returning/new account gets a first turn before already-selected eligible accounts. There is no new persistent allocation or quota ledger.

An account must have a running engine, current enabled central assignment, more than 30 seconds of credential life, no future projected cooldown, and current engine evidence for the requested model. The engine excludes disabled credentials, live provider cooldowns, exhausted configured local windows, active recovery probes and queued/running/streaming jobs. Actual admission still needs the existing central lease and durable execution binding.

A synchronous dispatch reservation prevents concurrent HTTP requests from choosing the same account before engine admission begins. Each release is bound to its reservation token; a repeated/stale callback cannot release a newer reservation. Standard's execution policy then holds one account slot from the start of admission through job completion and the settlement attempt. The engine also has concurrency one. A `202` response, disconnected reader or status poll does not free an active job's slot. Unconfirmed terminal persistence blocks new admissions even when a local slot is released.

Rotation is work-conserving among healthy eligible accounts: a busy account waits while an idle healthy account may serve another request. With equal available healthy accounts, new requests cycle evenly; a single remaining healthy account can handle successive completed requests. Recovering accounts follow the separate probe budget below. This is request fairness, not equal token consumption, equal latency or guaranteed throughput. It adds no provider requests-per-hour assumption.

## Quota and recovery

- The first attributed quota error or exhausted provider-limit observation excludes that account immediately. All models share this exclusion. Standard counts at most one quota-failure escalation per job, including proactive limit updates and a terminal quota error. Fresh observations can extend the deadline without multiplying the same job's backoff. A later shorter hint, successful unrelated work or a newly idle slot cannot shorten a known deadline.
- Without a reported reset, the engine uses its existing 60-second exponential cooldown, capped at 30 minutes. After expiry, one real authorized request per account can test recovery, subject to the pool budget below; no background generation or same-request retry runs. At that admission, expired reset-less zero observations are retired so they cannot reopen the old cooldown before the probe runs. Fresh zero evidence during the probe starts/extends a new exclusion, even if that job produces successful output.
- Confirmed successful recovery clears the failure state only when no future cooldown remains. Completion cleanup preserves that success even when finishing the central grant synchronously aborts its signal. Standard checkpoints both exclusion and confirmed recovery, and records last use/request-limit consumption when an account is acquired. The credential-bound checkpoint remains best-effort local recovery evidence; central grants and durable execution records provide execution authority. A filesystem failure is not proof of a saved checkpoint.
- An explicit central admission denial backs off that account for 60 seconds, doubling on repeated denials to 30 minutes. Successful acquire/bind clears this local denial history. These process-local observations are not a copied budget balance, a provider reset estimate or a guarantee across restart. Central remains authoritative after restart. Transport errors do not manufacture quota evidence.
- The ten-second central reconciliation updates cooldown/model observations even when the operator revision is unchanged. Pause, remove, credential revision and epoch changes retain their existing fencing and engine-stop behavior.

No eligible account returns `503 service_busy` with a fixed `Retry-After: 5` before an engine attempt. Five seconds is a caller polling hint, not a promise that quota resets then. The response contains no account counts, identities, reasons or reset diagnostics. Do not respond to saturation by borrowing Premium, clearing provider cooldowns or creating new keys for uncertain work.

## Recovery admission budget

Standard allows at most one new recovery selection every five seconds across its models and accounts. The limiter uses a monotonic clock, so wall-clock corrections cannot advance it. Recovery means an engine retaining quota/repeated-failure state after its wait, or an account returning from explicit central admission denial. Account removal/reordering and failed forwarding do not refund or reset this process-local budget. A runtime restart creates a new local budget; central ownership, persisted provider cooldowns and account grants still apply.

Healthy candidates remain available while the recovery interval is closed. When the interval opens, normal rotation chooses among eligible healthy and recovering accounts; it does not generate a synthetic probe. Long recovery jobs may overlap on different accounts if their starts are at least five seconds apart, with the one-job-per-account limit still enforced. If only recovering accounts remain, excess requests receive the same bounded busy response. This trades recovery speed for bounded bursts, without a queue, retries, peer fallback or a fabricated provider quota.

## Identity and operator workflow

Original-key lookup precedes selection. Replaying an accepted text request uses its original job/account/epoch; changed payloads conflict. A duplicate in progress cannot remove the first request's lock. Engine failure, lost acknowledgements, missing engines and old epochs never cause redispatch. Images, Sites and provider-hosted tools remain forbidden before Standard selection. Client-owned function calling is text inference; each model continuation receives normal scheduling and central authorization.

After a delayed lookup, the runtime rechecks that its central projection is non-future and younger than 20 seconds before dispatching new work. If the caller disconnected during lookup, it releases request bookkeeping without selecting an account, taking a recovery turn or reserving execution. A disconnect after forwarding remains uncertain accepted work and follows the original identity/settlement contract; it is not permission to cancel and redispatch.

The existing private monitor's Health and Status filters remain useful for central account observations. Its **No issues detected** label is not live idle-slot capacity. Local engine cooldowns and admission backoff are not a new monitor telemetry feed. A busy response alone does not justify renaming, removing or re-registering an account. Respect known cooldowns; repair sign-in only for actual expiry/access problems; add capacity through manual registration of an eligible free account in Standard. See [quota operations](../maintainers/RELAY_QUOTA_AND_FLEET.md).

## Established patterns and tradeoffs

[NGINX](https://nginx.org/en/docs/http/ngx_http_upstream_module.html) combines round-robin distribution with per-upstream concurrency and failure controls. [Envoy](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/intro/arch_overview/upstream/load_balancing/load_balancers.html) describes least-request selection, and its [outlier detection](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/intro/arch_overview/upstream/outlier) excludes failing hosts for bounded periods. Those are the reference patterns for local dispatch reservation, busy-account exclusion and recovery backoff here; this implementation does not copy their automatic retries, backup hosts or availability exceptions. A small pool of at most 128 projected accounts permits a full eligibility scan.

[Envoy slow start](https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/intro/arch_overview/upstream/load_balancing/slow_start) illustrates gradual traffic return, and [HAProxy](https://www.haproxy.com/documentation/haproxy-configuration-tutorials/proxying-essentials/configuration-basics/backends/) documents rotation among available servers. Standard adapts those ideas with a bounded recovery-start rate and an ordered fair rotation. It does not implement Envoy's weight ramp or claim to measure a free account's remaining provider budget.

Premium retains its existing matching-engine cursor and admission concurrency. Standard accepts lower per-account concurrency to limit bursts on free accounts. Neither pool can lend capacity to the other.

## Verification and rollout

`npm run test:relay:services` includes real local HTTP/WebSocket fixtures for fair ordering, concurrent selection, one ongoing job per account, quota feedback/checkpoint recovery, unchanged-revision projection updates, central denials, duplicate/replay safety, failed persistence, forbidden workloads and Premium compatibility. Recovery coverage includes clock jumps, damaged history, stale releases, paced recovery with healthy traffic, repeated quota frames, late reset hints, confirmed recovery, fresh limits during successful output, abandoned lookup and stale/future readiness. Shared engine quota/public authorization regressions and a production build complement those fixtures; no test spends provider quota.

Use the reviewed local image manifest and [service runbook](../runbooks/relay-pool-services.md) for installation. Preserve the Standard state volume and wait for central ownership handoff when replacing its runtime. Do not activate gates as part of an image update. A successful build/install is separate from manual free-account registration and real provider workload qualification. Rollback restores the preceding reviewed Standard image and keeps state, central assignment and unknown executions; it restores the older selection behavior and is not a way to reset quota.
