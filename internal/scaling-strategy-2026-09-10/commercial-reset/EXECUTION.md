# Execution and investment gates

This plan replaces an open-ended catalog expansion with a small commercial test and the minimum implementation needed to deliver it honestly. Commercial discovery can proceed using clearly labeled planning assumptions. The fixed-price Sites path now has local billing, response-boundary and recovery verification; general inference findings retain their own scope. Live expansion requires the corresponding acceptance evidence. Dates below are elapsed windows, not delivery promises.

## First decisions

Adopt separate funding for the service and compute as the working commercial policy. Use the existing relay as the sole model supplier for the selected Sites workflow. A second supplier is a separate future continuity project when justified by funded obligations; it is not an implementation or activation prerequisite for this strategy. Defer an all-model subscription, broad modality additions, unproven wholesale guarantees, enterprise SLA promises and recurring free compute as growth tactics.

Treat $299/month and $750 setup as a test offer. Do not alter existing customer balances or silently replace published terms. Preserve the restored image studio. Keep the new strategy private until capabilities and prospective terms are ready for an application-copy change.

## Sites implementation and rollout

The fixed-price content path is complete in the local working tree: quote acceptance, configurable price/rolling allowance, an explicit cohort, wallet reservations, one provider attempt, durable receipts and original-price recovery. Do not require an exact supplier quota, invented token usage or a passing supplier-ceiling assertion to finish or enable this mode.

Deploy compatible worker/federation/application versions with new generation paused, preserve original state, verify the exact environment and enable the selected cohort under [the operations procedure](../../../docs/maintainers/SITES_OPERATIONS.md#rollout). Native-host save and private publication require their own original acknowledgements. Direct CLI archive upload remains a separate qualification task and is not the current successful save path.

The $299/month agency offer below remains proposed business discovery. The $0.05 per completed content generation is an implemented provisional tariff, not that subscription's price or included compute. Measure completed delivery, support, failed/unknown operations and available supplier expense to revise future scope; local verification does not prove profitability or live paid activation.

Cap discovery at approximately 20 founder hours before the first explicit go/no-go decision; this is a planning budget, not a claim that all engineering remediation fits that time.

## Discovery before major construction

Use ten qualified conversations as an initial evidence window. Qualification means the prospect already operates several recurring client projects and can identify a person who owns the expense. Do not count supportive friends, signups or users who only want discounted inference as validated demand. Record reasons for rejection as carefully as positive feedback.

Ask for the last real month-close or budget incident: what happened, who handled it, what tools were used and how much recurring effort it took. Then demonstrate the proposed workflow and compare it with the prospect's best credible substitute. Ask whether the proposed $299 monthly fee and $750 setup are worth paying at that scope, and what would prevent renewal. No messages or invitations were sent by this audit; this is a prepared interview guide.

Seek at least three explicit paid-pilot commitments from the ten qualified conversations before building agency-specific expansion features. A written commitment is useful discovery evidence but is weaker than payment and use. Do not take payment for unavailable guarantees; any limited paid discovery/setup engagement needs an explicit deliverable and availability date. If the threshold fails, revise the job once from recorded objections or stop the wedge before more feature work.

## Days 8–30: prove one closed cycle

Implement only the smallest missing elements needed for approved client budgets, scoped access, request attribution, a period statement and safe project closure/handover. The complete flow may initially include disclosed human assistance. Track the time. Prefer exports over a custom accounting integration and reviewed owner changes over a complex proration system.

Activate at most five paid pilots after the safety gates pass. Assign each a recorded traffic cap, tested concurrency limit, funding ceiling, owner and service version. Keep existing customers' limits and terms distinct. Do not use these pilots to justify universal availability or mature enterprise readiness.

The day-30 deliverable is evidence, not a feature count: activated paying accounts, completed workflow steps, matched statements, actual cost per account, support effort, incidents, and buyer feedback. Failure to activate because a technical prerequisite is unfinished is not evidence that customers rejected the value proposition; distinguish delivery failure from commercial rejection.

## Days 31–60: renewal and cost

Require at least three of the five pilots to pay for a second cycle at the intended recurring price. Record the lost accounts and their reasons. For each retained agency, measure recurring effort saved against its real alternative, not a universal industry baseline. A willing testimonial or increased token use alone does not establish retained software value.

Calculate contribution including actual delivery labor and payment/incident costs. Target at least 75% platform contribution in the repeatable operating case and average recurring delivery near or below half an hour per account at the proposed request envelope. Treat unusual support cases explicitly; do not hide them outside the cost ledger. If costs exceed the model, reduce future scope, automate repeated work or test a higher price before adding accounts.

## Days 61–90: choose a business

Continue only if the paid renewals, repeatable workflow and actual cost support the offer. Broaden cautiously toward ten retained agencies; that remains early evidence, not a statistically established retention curve. Expand acquisition only when recorded CAC and observed retained contribution support cash payback within six months. The six-month threshold is a management constraint for this plan, not an industry statistic.

If customers buy only setup, offer a separately scoped implementation service and stop describing it as software retention. If they buy only discounted compute, maintain funded PAYG where viable and abandon the agency premium. If customers value the workflow but a competitor serves it adequately, consider integration or a smaller product instead of rebuilding all gateway infrastructure. If no repeatable profitable job emerges, freeze expansion and operate within existing funded demand.

## Engineering acceptance before paid operational claims

| Capability | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Client budget ownership | Unauthorized client/member cannot read or raise another budget; role change, revocation and handover take effect correctly |
| Spending enforcement | Parallel requests reserve within the most restrictive applicable budget; datastore failure fails safely; retries cannot create extra authority |
| Durable accounting | Request and attempt identity survive crashes; settlement is idempotent; late usage, partial results, refunds and period rollover reconcile |
| Customer statements | Every billable event belongs to the correct project and payer; totals reproduce ledger entries; late adjustments are visible and linked |
| Traffic admission | Shared monthly allowance, request bounds and concurrent capacity are separate enforced controls; one client cannot consume another's authority |
| Safe provider recovery | Narrow compatibility tests, quote preservation, bounded attempt cost and no automatic replay of ambiguous side effects |
| Public-information boundary | Allowlisted HTTP/stream/async fields; administrator authorization for diagnostics; reencoded image artifacts; no internal mechanism in public copy or assets |
| Commercial activation | Versioned plan agreement, actual active entitlement, failed payment/cancellation/renewal behavior and existing balance handling agree |
| Release | Relevant adversarial and authorization suites, production build, rendered affected-page checks and browser-asset scan pass |

These extend the earlier technical tickets; they do not prescribe SQL or claim all mechanisms are missing. Review current implementations before changing database or routing code. Preserve existing tested safeguards and avoid running diagnostic probes with production credentials.

## Investment limits

For a new provider, separate one-time integration/evaluation cost, monthly maintenance, outage testing and any minimum spend. Let `C` be expected recurring contribution from the paid customers needing that capability, `M` monthly maintenance and `K` one-time work. The proposal should show positive `C − M` and an explicit recovery period for `K`, or identify a funded reliability obligation that justifies the expense independently. Do not count total customer spend as incremental benefit.

As an illustrative cost check, $3,000 integration and $300/month maintenance with $50 incremental monthly contribution per adopter requires more than six adopters merely to cover maintenance; it requires more to recover the initial work. Those figures are hypothetical. Any later backup proposal is an insurance decision with its own cash budget; the accepted Sites workflow continues through the existing relay only.

Defer additional provider adapters, custom identity features, dedicated deployments, bespoke dashboards, automated customer payouts and new consumer applications until a paying need and supported contribution justify each. A feature enters the roadmap with a customer job, owner, cost envelope, acceptance evidence and exit criterion.

## Scorecard

Use a weekly private scorecard with paying agencies, active client projects, service MRR excluding compute/setup, renewals, contribution dollars, delivery hours, CAC, unrestricted cash coverage, unresolved financial events and failed service objectives. Link each entry to a dated source record. Model request counts, free signups and catalog size are operational context, not substitutes for these measures.

The decisive next action is to complete the financial baseline and prepare the narrow priced demonstration while closing the already reproduced accounting/public-response gaps. Do not launch a more generous subscription while those inputs remain unknown.
